# Plan of the book

The book has four parts and twelve chapters. Chapters 1–3 are drafted; the rest are planned.

## Part I. The idea

1. **The swarm that chooses a home.** A swarm of bees picks a new nest without any bee comparing the options. The decision lives in the pattern of their exchanges, and that is the book's central premise.
2. **Information is physical.** From Maxwell's demon to the experiments that confirmed Landauer's limit: bits have an energy price, so intelligence can be measured with the tools of physics.
3. **What is an agent?** Thermostats, neurons, bees and a slime mould. Why a gas and a crystal are not minds, and what seizures and anaesthesia reveal about the conditions for thinking.

## Part II. The measures

4. **A ruler and a clock for minds.** Dimensional analysis applied to thought: crossing times, bandwidth through a surface, and why catching a ball takes a brain of a particular size.
5. **How much does a mind need to know?** Intelligence as efficiency, achievement per bit. Twenty questions, children learning words, and machines that need ten thousand times more data.
6. **The limits.** Energy, wiring and the speed of light set ceilings on any mind, including a mind the size of a planet.

## Part III. Minds inside minds

7. **The funnel.** Every agent takes in a flood and passes on a trickle. Humans see at a billion bits per second and act at ten. What that means for the minds built from us.
8. **The same algorithm everywhere.** Bees and neurons decide the same way. The wisdom of crowds, and how it fails when everyone listens to everyone.
9. **Fractals and breaks.** Starling flocks, neuronal avalanches and the wiring of brains and chips. Finding where one agent ends and the next level begins.

## Part IV. The edges

10. **Where is the self?** Split brains, conjoined twins, and why a society can be intelligent without being anyone.
11. **An intelligent Schrödinger's cat.** Facts that are actual for one agent and not yet for another, and an experiment that could tell.
12. **How we could be wrong.** The predictions gathered in one place, with the observations that would refute them.
