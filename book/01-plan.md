# Plan of the book

The book has four parts and fifteen chapters. Chapters 1 to 6, 10 and 13 are drafted; the rest are planned.

The argument runs in four moves. Part I says what a mind made of agents is. Part II gives it measures: time, efficiency, meaning and limits. Part III climbs the hierarchy and asks what changes, and what stays the same, from level to level. Part IV follows the theory to its edges: the self, the planet, the galaxy and the quantum world.

## Part I. The idea

1. **The swarm that chooses a home.** *(drafted)* A swarm of bees picks a new nest without any bee comparing the options. The decision lives in the pattern of their exchanges, and that is the book's central premise.
2. **Information is physical.** *(drafted)* From Maxwell's demon to the experiments that confirmed Landauer's limit: bits have an energy price, so intelligence can be measured with the tools of physics.
3. **What is an agent?** *(drafted)* Thermostats, neurons, bees and a slime mould. Why a gas and a crystal are not minds, and what seizures and anaesthesia reveal about the conditions for thinking.

## Part II. The measures

4. **A ruler and a clock for minds.** *(drafted)* Dimensional analysis applied to thought: crossing times, bandwidth through a surface, and why catching a ball takes a brain of a particular size.
5. **How much does a mind need to know?** *(drafted)* Intelligence as efficiency, achievement per bit. Twenty questions, children learning words, and machines that need ten thousand times more data. Why intelligence has a maximum, and what a superintelligence could and could not be.
6. **The glance.** *(drafted)* Bits are not meaning. What a message conveys depends on what sender and receiver already share. The context we are born with. A relationship as a compression algorithm, communication by exception, and why perfect understanding carries no news. The couple as a joint intelligence, and belonging as what a larger mind feels like from inside.
7. **The limits.** Energy, wiring and the speed of light set ceilings on any mind.

## Part III. Minds inside minds

8. **The funnel.** Every agent takes in a flood and passes on a trickle. Humans see at a billion bits per second and act at ten. What that means for the minds built from us.
9. **The same algorithm everywhere.** Bees and neurons decide the same way. The wisdom of crowds, and how it fails when everyone listens to everyone.
10. **The same thought at a different speed.** *(drafted)* Wind tunnels, scale models and cognitive time. A brain and a swarm decide in the same number of their own steps. How slow minds think about fast things. How many thoughts a mind gets in its lifetime, from a person to a galaxy.
11. **Fractals and breaks.** Starling flocks, neuronal avalanches and the wiring of brains and chips. Finding where one agent ends and the next level begins.

## Part IV. The edges

12. **Where is the self?** Split brains, conjoined twins, and why a society can be intelligent without being anyone.
13. **Minds the size of worlds.** *(drafted)* Humanity as a federated mind whose modules are languages. Every species as a slow mind, and speciation as a mind dividing. The language a larger mind must speak, and an experiment for listening to humanity. Why nations are not minds, why the Earth could be one, and what a galactic intelligence would have to be like.
14. **An intelligent Schrödinger's cat.** Facts that are actual for one agent and not yet for another, and an experiment that could tell.
15. **How we could be wrong.** The predictions gathered in one place, with the observations that would refute them.
