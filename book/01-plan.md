# Plan of the book

The book has five parts and nineteen chapters. All nineteen now exist in draft.

The argument runs in five moves. Part I says what a mind made of agents is. Part II gives it four measures: time and space, efficiency, meaning and capacity. Part III climbs the hierarchy: what kinds of mind there are, what holds a mind together, what stays the same from level to level, and how to find the levels. Part IV states the theory compactly, as a physicist would, and then runs it forwards: from a task to the shape of the mind that can perform it, and then to the artificial minds now being built. Part V follows the theory to its edges: the self, the planet and the galaxy, the machines now joining us, and the quantum world.

Two threads tie the parts together. The first is a tension between integration and independence: a collective must communicate enough to act together, but not so much that its members stop judging for themselves. Its practical rule is *share code, not beliefs*. The second is a law of unity: a collective is one for as long as the traffic inside it outruns some other rate. It acts as one while its signals outrun the world (Chapter 4), is a self while its internal traffic outruns its boundary traffic (Chapter 15), and stays one kind while its traffic outruns the drift of its code (Chapter 9).

## Part I. The idea

1. **The swarm that chooses a home.** *(drafted)* A swarm of bees picks a new nest without any bee comparing the options. The decision lives in the pattern of their exchanges, and that is the book's central premise. The wisdom of crowds, and how it fails when everyone copies everyone.
2. **Information is physical.** *(drafted)* From Maxwell's demon to the experiments that confirmed Landauer's limit: bits have an energy price, so intelligence can be measured with the tools of physics. The great funnel: a billion bits in, ten out.
3. **What is an agent?** *(drafted)* Thermostats, neurons, bees and a slime mould. Why a gas and a crystal are not minds, and what seizures and anaesthesia reveal about the conditions for thinking.

## Part II. The measures

4. **A ruler and a clock for minds.** *(drafted)* Time and space. Dimensional analysis applied to thought: crossing times, the coherence number, bandwidth through a surface, and why catching a ball takes a brain of a particular size.
5. **How much does a mind need to know?** *(drafted)* Efficiency. Intelligence as achievement per bit, a pure number with a ceiling of one. Twenty questions, children learning words, and machines that need ten thousand times more data. Protein folding, and the split between gathering the right bits and unlocking what they already hold. What a superintelligence could and could not be.
6. **The glance.** *(drafted)* Meaning. What a message conveys depends on what sender and receiver already share. The context we are born with. A relationship as a compression algorithm, communication by exception, and why perfect understanding carries no news. Beetles that counterfeit an ant colony's code, costly signals between rivals, and the couple as a joint intelligence.
7. **How much can a mind do?** *(drafted)* Capacity, the other half of intelligence = capacity × efficiency. Nine hours of chewing, the one percent of the brain you can afford to use, and why thin channels are cheaper than fast ones. Why a mind's whole grows more slowly than its parts, what the machines spend, and the most intelligent kilogram physics allows.

## Part III. Minds inside minds

8. **Two kinds of mind.** *(drafted)* Integrated minds, like brains, whose agents talk as fast as they think, and federated minds, like swarms and societies, whose agents think far more than they say. An octopus with eight semi-independent arms, why you can think only one thing at a time, ant colonies that cannot be overloaded, frigatebirds that sleep half a brain at a time while colonies rest in shifts, small teams that disrupt and large teams that develop, and why no mind can be large, fast and unified at once.
9. **How a mind divides.** *(drafted)* Unity as a ratio of rates. Every species as a slow mind, a warbler that no longer recognises its own song, languages that split the way species do, aircraft factories that forget how to build their own planes when turnover outruns training, and the tree of life as a family tree of minds.
10. **The same thought at a different speed.** *(drafted)* Wind tunnels, scale models and cognitive time. A brain and a swarm decide in the same number of their own steps. How slow minds think about fast things. How many thoughts a mind gets in its lifetime, from a person to a galaxy.
11. **Fractals and breaks.** *(drafted)* The coast of Britain. Starling flocks, neuronal avalanches and the wiring of brains and chips. Why minds are self-similar, and how the places where the pattern breaks show where one agent ends and the next level begins. Why animals slow down as they grow and cities speed up.

## Part IV. The theory at work

12. **The theory on one page.** *(drafted)* The framework stated as physics: four base dimensions, a dozen variables, twelve pure numbers, five limits, the scaling relations and the nine relations the theory claims. An honest table of which quantities can be measured today, and what is still missing. The only chapter with formulas.
13. **Designing a mind.** *(drafted)* The theory run forwards. Given a task, ten million bits per second in and one decision per second out, derive the width, depth, size and kind of the mind that can do it, and compare with the human visual system. Why most of a brain is library, not pipeline. When the answer is a swarm, and why every team has a best size. The map of possible minds, and what each kind cannot do.

14. **The artificial mind.** *(drafted)* Modern artificial intelligence on the book's ruler. Why a language model commits to one continuation at a time, how it compares with a brain in capacity, efficiency, energy and context, why missing bodily context breeds literal misreading and prompt hijacking, the four limits the theory foresees, and the directions it predicts: sparse, frugal, federated, sharing a code, or coupling internal states directly. Why there will be no single superintelligence.

## Part V. The edges

15. **Where is the self?** *(drafted)* Split brains, conjoined twins, couples and nations. Why a society can be intelligent without being anyone. The theory's position on consciousness, and belonging as what a larger mind feels like from inside.
16. **Minds the size of worlds.** *(drafted)* Humanity as a federated mind whose modules are languages. The language a larger mind must speak, why prices keep rival strangers honest, and an experiment for listening to humanity. Why nations are not minds, why the Earth could be one, and what a galactic intelligence would have to be like.
17. **The new agents.** *(drafted)* Machines joining the collective: what happens to humanity's mind when agents that outtalk humans and counterfeit human signals at near-zero cost become a large part of humanity's conversation.
18. **An intelligent Schrödinger's cat.** *(drafted)* Facts that are actual for one agent and not yet for another, and an experiment that could tell.
19. **How we could be wrong.** *(drafted)* The predictions gathered in one place, with the observations that would refute them, and the design rule the theory leaves us with.
