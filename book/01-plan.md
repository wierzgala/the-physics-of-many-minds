# Plan of the book

The book has four parts and sixteen chapters. Chapters 1 to 6, 9, 10 and 13 are drafted; the rest are planned.

The argument runs in four moves. Part I says what a mind made of agents is. Part II gives it four measures: time and space, efficiency, meaning and capacity. Part III climbs the hierarchy: what kinds of mind there are, what holds a mind together, what stays the same from level to level, and how to find the levels. Part IV follows the theory to its edges: the self, the planet and the galaxy, the machines now joining us, and the quantum world.

Two threads tie the parts together. The first is a tension between integration and independence: a collective must communicate enough to act together, but not so much that its members stop judging for themselves. Its practical rule is *share code, not beliefs*. The second is a law of unity: a collective is one for as long as the traffic inside it outruns some other rate. It acts as one while its signals outrun the world (Chapter 4), is a self while its internal traffic outruns its boundary traffic (Chapter 12), and stays one kind while its traffic outruns the drift of its code (Chapter 9).

## Part I. The idea

1. **The swarm that chooses a home.** *(drafted)* A swarm of bees picks a new nest without any bee comparing the options. The decision lives in the pattern of their exchanges, and that is the book's central premise. The wisdom of crowds, and how it fails when everyone copies everyone.
2. **Information is physical.** *(drafted)* From Maxwell's demon to the experiments that confirmed Landauer's limit: bits have an energy price, so intelligence can be measured with the tools of physics. The great funnel: a billion bits in, ten out.
3. **What is an agent?** *(drafted)* Thermostats, neurons, bees and a slime mould. Why a gas and a crystal are not minds, and what seizures and anaesthesia reveal about the conditions for thinking.

## Part II. The measures

4. **A ruler and a clock for minds.** *(drafted)* Time and space. Dimensional analysis applied to thought: crossing times, the coherence number, bandwidth through a surface, and why catching a ball takes a brain of a particular size.
5. **How much does a mind need to know?** *(drafted)* Efficiency. Intelligence as achievement per bit, a pure number with a ceiling of one. Twenty questions, children learning words, and machines that need ten thousand times more data. What a superintelligence could and could not be.
6. **The glance.** *(drafted)* Meaning. What a message conveys depends on what sender and receiver already share. The context we are born with. A relationship as a compression algorithm, communication by exception, and why perfect understanding carries no news. The couple as a joint intelligence.
7. **How much can a mind do?** Capacity, the other half of intelligence = capacity × efficiency. What energy, wiring and the cost of size allow a mind to process per second, and why bigger is not always faster.

## Part III. Minds inside minds

8. **Two kinds of mind.** Integrated minds, like brains, whose agents talk as fast as they think, and federated minds, like swarms and societies, whose agents think far more than they say. The funnel as the mechanism that separates them, and what each kind is good at.
9. **How a mind divides.** *(drafted)* Unity as a ratio of rates. Every species as a slow mind, a warbler that no longer recognises its own song, languages that split the way species do, and the tree of life as a family tree of minds.
10. **The same thought at a different speed.** *(drafted)* Wind tunnels, scale models and cognitive time. A brain and a swarm decide in the same number of their own steps. How slow minds think about fast things. How many thoughts a mind gets in its lifetime, from a person to a galaxy.
11. **Fractals and breaks.** Starling flocks, neuronal avalanches and the wiring of brains and chips. Finding where one agent ends and the next level begins.

## Part IV. The edges

12. **Where is the self?** Split brains, conjoined twins, couples and nations. Why a society can be intelligent without being anyone. The theory's position on consciousness, and belonging as what a larger mind feels like from inside.
13. **Minds the size of worlds.** *(drafted)* Humanity as a federated mind whose modules are languages. The language a larger mind must speak, and an experiment for listening to humanity. Why nations are not minds, why the Earth could be one, and what a galactic intelligence would have to be like.
14. **The new agents.** Machines joining the collective. Why a superintelligence cannot beat the ceiling on efficiency, why a machine shares our culture but not our genome, and what happens when agents that bypass the ten-bit funnel become a large part of humanity's conversation.
15. **An intelligent Schrödinger's cat.** Facts that are actual for one agent and not yet for another, and an experiment that could tell.
16. **How we could be wrong.** The predictions gathered in one place, with the observations that would refute them, and the design rule the theory leaves us with.
